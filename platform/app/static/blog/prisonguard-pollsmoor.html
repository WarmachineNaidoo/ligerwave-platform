The Riot They Said Came From Nowhere

PrisonGuard • Predicting riots before they start
June 2026 • 5 min read • Based on incident reports from Pollsmoor Prison, 2022

At 14:03 on a Tuesday afternoon, inmates in Pollsmoor's C Section began what investigators would later describe as a "spontaneous riot." The phrase appears in correctional incident reports across the world: spontaneous. Meaning there was no warning. Meaning nobody saw it coming.

But riots don't happen spontaneously. They are preceded by signals that are invisible to the human eye but measurable in radio waves.

"Spontaneous riot" is what investigators write when they have no data. We set out to give them data.

In the 30 minutes before a riot: heart rates across the block rise by an average of 22%. Inmates cluster in groups larger than normal. Pacing — a known pre-agitation behaviour — increases in frequency by 300%. Crowding occurs as multiple inmates gather in a single cell. All of these signals are detectable through WiFi CSI.

PrisonGuard is not a camera. It does not see faces. It does not record conversations. It measures the aggregate physiological state of every person in a prison block — through concrete walls, without any wearable device — and calculates a riot probability score in real-time.

At 60% probability, the system alerts the control room. At 80%, it recommends pre-deployment of response teams. By the time the first chair is thrown, correctional officers are already in position.

South Africa's correctional facilities reported 27 major incidents in 2023. Every one was classified as "spontaneous." With PrisonGuard, that classification becomes obsolete — not because riots won't happen, but because you'll see them coming.
