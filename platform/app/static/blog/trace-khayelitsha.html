The Room Nobody Could See Into

Trace • Through-wall intelligence for tactical operations
June 2026 • 4 min read

When a SAPS tactical team responds to a barricaded suspect, a hostage situation, or a potential terrorist threat, they share one critical blind spot: they cannot see through walls.

Cameras can't. Audio listening devices can't. Thermal imaging can't penetrate concrete. Every door breach is a leap of faith — the team knows roughly where the subject is, but does not know their physiological state. Are they asleep? Agitated? Injured? Under the influence? Each scenario demands a different approach.

"SAPS is the most sophisticated police service on the continent. But in 2025, they were still breaching rooms blind."

Trace uses WiFi CSI to answer one question before a door opens: what is the heart rate of the person inside? A rate below 70 BPM suggests calm or sleep. 70-100 BPM suggests alertness. Above 100 BPM suggests high stress, panic, or physical exertion. This information — delivered to an officer's earpiece as an audio tone — changes the tactical picture from "someone is inside" to "someone is inside, and I know their state."

No cameras. No audio recording. No privacy violation. Just a single physiological data point that can save lives on both sides of the door.
